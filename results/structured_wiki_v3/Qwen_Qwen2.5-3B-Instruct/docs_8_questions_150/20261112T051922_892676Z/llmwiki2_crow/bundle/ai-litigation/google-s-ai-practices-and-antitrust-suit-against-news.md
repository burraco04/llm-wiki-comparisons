# Google's AI Practices and Antitrust Suit Against News Publishers

## Overview
A class action lawsuit filed by news publishers accuses Google and Alphabet of anticompetitive practices, particularly regarding AI technologies used in Google's Search Generative Experience (SGE) and Bard AI chatbot. The lawsuit alleges misuse of publishers' content and ad revenue, and seeks an injunction to prevent improper use of publisher data for AI training.

## Key Points
- **AI Training Data**: Google's Bard AI was trained on datasets including news, magazine, and digital publications, as reported by the News Media Alliance and a Washington Post article. The Post found that News and Media sites were the third largest category of AI training data.
- **Impact on Publishers**: The lawsuit addresses the potential future impact of AI on publishers' businesses, including a 75% chance that AI will answer queries without requiring a click-through to publishers' websites.
- **Specific Claims**:
  - Misuse of publishers' content and ad revenue.
  - Improper spoliation of evidence, including the destruction of chat messages.
  - Request for an injunction requiring Google to obtain consent from publishers to use their website data for AI training and to allow publishers who opt out of Search Engine Guidelines to still appear in Google search results.
- **Legal Context**:
  - The lawsuit invokes the Sherman Act and Clayton Act for class-wide monetary and injunctive relief.
  - Google denies the lawsuit's merit.
