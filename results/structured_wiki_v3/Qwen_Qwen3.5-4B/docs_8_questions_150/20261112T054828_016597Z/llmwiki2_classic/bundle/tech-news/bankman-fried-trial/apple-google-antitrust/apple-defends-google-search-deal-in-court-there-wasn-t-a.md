Eddy Cue testified in US v. Google, the antitrust trial over Google's search business, defending the Information Services Agreement (ISA) that makes Google the default search engine on Apple devices. The ISA has been in place since 2002, with Cue negotiating the current iteration with Google CEO Sundar Pichai in 2016. During renegotiation, Apple sought a higher percentage of revenue from users directed to the search engine, while Pichai wanted to keep the deal as is; they compromised on an undisclosed number. Cue stated there was no valid alternative to Google at the time and there still isn't one. He argued the deal was about more than economics and that Apple never seriously considered switching providers or building its own search product. Apple also argues that switching search engines is easy, contradicting claims by competitors like DuckDuckGo. Additionally, Apple claims better privacy than Google, noting that Safari was made harder to track, and downplays Google payments as a profit driver, citing platform appeal instead. The Department of Justice questioned whether Google is the best choice or simply pays the most, asking what the market looks like without payments.
