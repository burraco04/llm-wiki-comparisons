Sam Bankman-Fried (SBF) faces trial at Manhattan Federal Court for orchestrating the collapse of FTX, a crypto exchange he founded, accused of one of the biggest financial frauds in American history. SBF pleaded not-guilty to seven charges of fraud and conspiracy.

Michael Lewis's book "Going Infinite" is set to debut on the first day of the trial. In an interview on 60 Minutes, Lewis described SBF as having gone from having nothing to having $22.5 billion, becoming the richest person in the world under 30. Lewis and SBF met more than 100 times over two years. SBF raised approximately $2 billion, including $1 billion in 2021, from backers including Sequoia, Temasek, and Tiger Global.

FTX secured marketing deals with sports icons Tom Brady ($55 million) and Steph Curry ($35 million) to lend legitimacy. Trading volume climbed to $15 billion per day. Anna Wintour contacted SBF via Zoom to sponsor her Met Costume Ball. SBF's political fundraising strategy involved meeting with Mitch McConnell to fund Republican candidates offsetting Trump's influence. However, SBF reportedly offered $5 billion to Trump to cancel his presidential re-run bid.

The value of cryptocurrencies on FTX was not banked to much more than speculation and the promise of higher value, so when valuations fell, they fell very hard. SBF has been denied bail and is blamed by those who lost money in Alameda or FTX for causing a ripple effect of crypto woes. Ayelet Noff, CEO of Sliced Brand, criticized SBF for having no management experience, being over his head, and delaying crypto adoption by at least a year or two.
