The U.S. government's antitrust trial against Google stems from a 2020 claim regarding its dominance in the online search market, separate from a January filing by the Department of Justice and eight states. The government argues Google uses platforms and deals to block competition in search or advertising, hindering competitors from accessing data needed to improve products. Google argues its actions are legal business practices because being the default engine is desirable, not illegal.

Google CEO Sundar Pichai testified in the trial, defending tactics including deals to make Google the default search engine. He stated the decision to pay for defaults is made with the consumer in mind, allowing Google to collect data to be the best search engine across devices. Pichai admitted in 2018 attempting to get Tim Cook to preinstall Google on iOS devices to connect services, hoping for a win-win where Google gains search users/data and Apple gains revenue from a search agreement, but Cook declined as Apple does not preload third-party software.

Testimony revealed Google spent $26.3 billion in 2021 to make itself the default search engine across platforms. Google search head Prabhakar Raghavan testified on October 28 that Google paid $26.3 billion in 2021 to multiple browsers, phones, and platforms, including Apple, Samsung, and Mozilla. About $18 billion of the $26.3 billion total went directly to Apple; Google's deal with Apple has been in place since 2003. A 2007 Google product strategy meeting email showed that when users changed their browser homepage to Google, they did 15% more Google searches, dropping 27% when they switched away. Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella testified that Bing would have been competitive with Google had it made similar deals with Apple, stating he was willing to spend $15 billion per year to get Bing into Apple's default search.

Regarding data privacy, the DOJ accused Google of systematically destroying chats via a history-off option deleting messages every 24 hours. Pichai claimed he took action against the history-off default in February to comply with a litigation hold, though Bellshaw cited a 2021 message exchange where Pichai discussed a personnel matter unrelated to the hold.

In 2005, Microsoft made Internet Explorer the default search engine, prompting Google's legal chief David Drummond to send an angry letter accusing this of anticompetitive behavior. Bellshaw used Drummond's letter to highlight Google's hypocrisy regarding defaults, noting that changes to defaults worsen problems and most users do not change them, arguments also used by DuckDuckGo, Brave, and Bing against Google.

The case, expected to continue for several weeks, could result in outcomes similar to the 1990s Microsoft trial, requiring Google to change behavior, share APIs, ban exclusive deals with manufacturers, and turn over collected data to competitors. The DOJ notes Google gets 16 times more data than Bing daily, and a ruling may impact ongoing FTC cases against Amazon, Apple, and Facebook.
