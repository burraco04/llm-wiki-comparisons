The trial of Sam Bankman-Fried, former CEO of FTX, began with opening statements on the second day, following jury selection on Wednesday morning. The prosecution, led by Thane Rehn, characterized Bankman-Fried not as a 'crypto boy' genius but as a 'man' who stole billions from thousands of victims. Allegations included using Alameda Research (a crypto hedge fund he owned) and Caroline Ellison (CEO of Alameda and his girlfriend) as fronts to gain secret access to customer funds, directing employees to conceal money flows, forging financial documents, and lying to customers, lenders, and investors. The prosecution also noted his funded jet-setting lifestyle, political donations, and risky bets.

In contrast, the defense, led by Mark Cohen, argued that Bankman-Fried did not defraud anyone and presented him as a nerdy startup founder acting in 'good faith.' This narrative aimed to counter the prosecution's 'cartoon of a villain' portrayal, noting that Bankman-Fried had spent seven weeks in a Brooklyn prison prior to the trial. The defense claimed Alameda was a successful hedge fund operating legally as an FTX customer, payment processor, and market maker, rather than a Ponzi scheme.

Following the opening statements, the prosecution called witness Marc-Antoine Julliard, a Paris-born cocoa trader living in London who invested in FTX in 2021. Julliard testified about attempting to withdraw nearly $100,000 on November 8, 2021, which he never succeeded in doing. The prosecution also called Adam Yedidia, an MIT graduate and close friend of Bankman-Fried, who testified that he had legal immunity due to concerns that he had unwittingly written code contributing to a crime.
