Helena World Chronicle, LLC filed a class action lawsuit in the U.S. District Court in D.C. against Google and Alphabet, invoking the Sherman Act and Clayton Act to seek class-wide monetary and injunctive relief. The suit alleges that Google and Alphabet engage in anticompetitive behavior by siphoning off content, readers, and ad revenue through AI and older technologies.

The lawsuit specifically cites Google's Search Generative Experience (SGE), Bard AI chatbot, and the Knowledge Graph (launched in May 2012) as tools that worsen publisher harm by misappropriating content and shifting traffic away from news sites. By 2020, the Knowledge Graph contained 500 billion facts about 5 billion entities, much of which was derived from content misappropriated from publishers' websites and open source databases. Additionally, Google's Featured Snippets technology, which algorithmically extracts answers from webpages, is cited as a factor shifting traffic away from publishers' websites.

The case cites concerns over changing AdSense rates and Google's destruction of chat messages, an issue previously raised in the Epic Games antitrust lawsuit which Epic won. The suit seeks damages and an injunction requiring Google to obtain publisher consent to use website data for training AI products, including Google's and rivals', and allowing publishers opting out of SGE to appear in search results. The U.S. lawsuit follows a Google agreement with the Canadian government to pay Canadian media $73.5 million annually, distributed by headcount, after Meta began blocking Canadian news in August. The case arrives alongside the U.S. Justice Department's lawsuit against Google for monopolizing digital ad technologies, referencing a 2020 DOJ civil antitrust suit over search and search advertising. Plus cites a 2023 News Media Alliance report and a Washington Post article stating Google's Bard AI was trained on news, magazine, and digital publications, noting news sites were the third largest category of AI training data. Hausfeld law firm states Google's scheme causes profound harm to competition, consumers, labor, and a democratic free press.
