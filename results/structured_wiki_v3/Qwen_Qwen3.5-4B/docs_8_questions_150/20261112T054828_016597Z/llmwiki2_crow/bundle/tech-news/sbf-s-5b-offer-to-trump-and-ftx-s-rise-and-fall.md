Sam Bankman-Fried (SBF), the disgraced cryptocurrency entrepreneur accused of orchestrating the collapse of FTX, reportedly offered Donald Trump $5 billion to cancel his presidential re-run; no wire transfer was confirmed. SBF pleaded not guilty to seven charges of fraud and conspiracy in the Manhattan Federal Court trial.

Michael Lewis's book 'Going Infinite' is set to debut on the first day of SBF's trial. Lewis, introduced to SBF in early 2021 by a friend who wanted to back FTX, met SBF more than 100 times over the next two years. Lewis claimed SBF had $22.5 billion eighteen months earlier and was the richest person in the world under 30. 60 Minutes produced a segment featuring SBF shuffling cards and Lewis taking notes on a yellow legal pad. In a 60 Minutes interview, Lewis defended SBF's business practices, claiming FTX would have remained profitable without customer deposit runs.

FTX raised approximately $2 billion, including $1 billion in 2021, from backers including Sequoia, Temasek, and Tiger Global. To lend legitimacy, FTX secured marketing deals with Tom Brady ($55 million) and Steph Curry ($35 million). Anna Wintour contacted SBF via Zoom to sponsor her Met Costume Ball. FTX trading volume climbed to $15 billion per day. SBF also met with Mitch McConnell to fund Republican candidates offsetting Trump's influence.

The company's cryptocurrency valuations fell sharply after being based on speculation rather than banked value. SBF has been denied bail and is widely blamed by crypto investors for losses at Alameda Research and FTX. CEO Ayelet Noff of Sliced Brand criticized SBF, stating he lacked management experience, delayed crypto adoption by 1-2 years, and damaged the community. Financial discrepancies accumulated between FTX exchange and its sister company Alameda Research, leading to FTX's failure.
