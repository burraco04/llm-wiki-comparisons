On the second day of Sam Bankman-Fried's trial, jury selection concluded, and both sides presented opening statements before witnesses took the stand. Prosecutor Thane Rehn characterized Bankman-Fried as a 'man' who stole billions from thousands of victims, contrasting his public image as a 'crypto boy' genius. Rehn alleged that Bankman-Fried oversaw a thriving FTX exchange, traveled internationally, and socialized with celebrities like Tom Brady and Larry David while assuring customers their funds were safe. The prosecution claimed Bankman-Fried built this image on lies, allegedly stealing customer funds to finance a jet-setting lifestyle, donate millions to political candidates, and finance risky bets. Prosecutors argued Bankman-Fried used Alameda Research, a hedge fund he owned, and his girlfriend Caroline Ellison (CEO of Alameda) as a front to gain secret access to customer cash and crypto. They further alleged that Bankman-Fried directed employees to conceal money flows into FTX and forged financial documents for lenders and investors.

In response, defense lawyer Mark Cohen argued that Bankman-Fried did not defraud anyone and acted in 'good faith' as a nerdy startup founder, rejecting the prosecution's 'cartoon of a villain' narrative. The defense maintained that Alameda was a successful hedge fund and FTX an innovative company, not a Ponzi scheme, and claimed legal business practices were followed. Bankman-Fried compared building a startup to flying a plane, citing former Alameda CEO Caroline Ellison for failing to protect the fund from crypto market risks. He argued he was not guilty, stating he built two billion-dollar businesses and did not steal money, offering to give up personal wealth to make customers whole.

Witness testimony began with Marc-Antoine Julliard, a Paris-born cocoa trader living in London, who testified that he invested in FTX in 2021, trading Bitcoin and Dogecoin, and failed to withdraw almost $100,000 on November 8. Adam Yedidia, an MIT graduate and close friend of Bankman-Fried, testified that he joined Alameda and FTX after Bankman-Fried persuaded him to leave Jane Street; Yedidia claimed legal immunity because he feared his code contributed to a crime. The trial schedule indicated further witnesses were set to appear post-adjournment.
