Google's dominance is attributed to scale, longevity, ubiquity, and unmatched user data collection, which includes search queries, smart speaker inputs, and clicked ads or results for personalization. Its central index exceeds 100 million gigabytes, updated by machine learning to rank results by quality and usability rather than just keyword presence. Data volume has grown from approximately 9 ZB in 2013 to 120 ZB in 2023, driven by live information. While some queries yield more ads due to keyword matching, Google denies pushing users toward monetizable subjects or allowing ad systems to affect organic results, citing separate meaning matching processes for organic queries. Kent Walker argues Google is preferred due to quality, not default status, noting user efforts to switch defaults on Firefox and Windows. Controversies include allegations by Megan Gray that Google manipulated 'children's clothing' queries to promote 'NIKOLAI-brand kidswear' for ad revenue, a claim Google denied. Additionally, Wired removed an article on this manipulation; Grey, a former DuckDuckGo VP, admitted she may have misinterpreted evidence but maintained Google manipulates search to maximize revenue. Bing has attempted to compete with generative AI chatbot explanations but faced pricing failures, such as ignoring user constraints for bike prices.
