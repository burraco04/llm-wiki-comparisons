Helena World Chronicle, an Arkansas publisher owning two weekly newspapers, filed a class action lawsuit in U.S. District Court in D.C. against Google and Alphabet, alleging anticompetitive behavior under the Sherman Act. The suit claims Google siphons off publisher content, readers, and ad revenue through AI technologies and older methods like Featured Snippets and Search Generative Experience (SGE). The publisher argues that Google's Knowledge Graph, which launched in May 2012 and contained 500 billion facts by 2020, misappropriated data from publishers, causing them to lose billions of dollars. The case cites a 2023 News Media Alliance report and a Washington Post article stating Google's Bard AI was trained on news publications, which were the third-largest category of training data. The suit alleges changing AdSense rates and the improper spoliation of evidence via Google's destruction of chat messages, referencing the recent Epic Games antitrust win. It seeks damages and an injunction requiring Google to obtain publisher consent to use website data for training AI products, including rivals' AI, and allowing opt-out publishers to appear in search results. The U.S. lawsuit coincides with the Justice Department's suit against Google for monopolizing digital ad technologies. Plaintiff Helena World Chronicle, LLC invokes the Sherman Act and Clayton Act for class-wide monetary and injunctive relief. Hausfeld law firm states Google's scheme causes profound harm to competition, consumers, labor, and a democratic free press. Google recently reached an agreement with the Canadian government to pay $73.5 million annually to news organizations, though negotiations with Meta remain unresolved.
