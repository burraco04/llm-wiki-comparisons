# Apple defends Google Search deal in court: ‘There wasn’t a valid alternative’

## Core Content

In the landmark US v. Google antitrust trial, Apple witness Eddy Cue testified that Google Search was the only viable alternative for Apple devices, despite the Justice Department's focus on the lucrative Information Services Agreement (ISA). Cue argued that Apple prioritizes user experience and privacy, claiming Google offers the best product and that switching engines is seamless, while acknowledging Apple's belief in Google's privacy policies even as they criticize Google's own practices.

## Key Points

- Eddy Cue testified that there was no valid alternative to Google Search for Apple devices at the time of the deal.
- The Information Services Agreement (ISA) makes Google the default search engine on Apple products in exchange for billions in payments.
- Apple believes in protecting user privacy but also maintains a strong partnership with Google, creating a central tension in the trial.
- Cue argued that showing users alternative search engines during setup would degrade the user experience and slow down device onboarding.
- The Justice Department questions whether Apple's choice of Google is based on product quality or financial incentives.

## Facts and Data

- The trial is titled US v. Google.
- Eddy Cue is a witness for Apple.
- Judge Amit Mehta presided over the case.
- The Information Services Agreement (ISA) has been in place since 2002.
- Cue negotiated the current ISA iteration in 2016.
- Google CEO Sundar Pichai was involved in the 2016 renegotiation.
- Meagan Bellshaw is a Justice Department lawyer.
- John Schmidtlein is a Google lawyer.
- Three iPhone screenshots were shown regarding setup process.
- Screenshots included the Appearance screen, location-tracking prompt, and App Tracking Transparency pop-up.
- DuckDuckGo's CEO testified previously claiming switching search engines takes too many steps.
- Safari was introduced in early 2003.
- Before 2003, users had to manually type google.com to search.
- Apple deals with Yahoo and Bing to make those services easy to find.
- The ISA represents a significant portion of Apple's profits.
- Specific revenue numbers were discussed in closed court sessions.
- Google pays Apple billions of dollars a year.
- Apple believes it has better privacy than Google.
- The ISA requires Google to allow searching without logging in.
- Apple makes it harder for Google to track users via Safari.
- The courtroom had a monitor malfunction during testimony.
- Judge Mehta joked about Apple making a donation if they wanted to switch.
- Cue stated Apple would never consider walking away from the deal if revenue share couldn't be agreed upon.
- Cue claimed he couldn't remember the names of some search engine alternatives.
