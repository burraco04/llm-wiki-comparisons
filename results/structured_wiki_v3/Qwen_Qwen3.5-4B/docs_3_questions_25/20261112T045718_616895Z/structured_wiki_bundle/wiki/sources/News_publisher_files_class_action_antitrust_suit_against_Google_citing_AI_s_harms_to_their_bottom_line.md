# News publisher files class action antitrust suit against Google, citing AI’s harms to their bottom line

## Core Content

Helena World Chronicle, LLC has filed a class action antitrust lawsuit against Google and Alphabet in the U.S. District Court in D.C., alleging that Google's AI technologies, including Search Generative Experience (SGE) and Bard, along with older features like the Knowledge Graph, are siphoning content, readers, and ad revenue from news publishers. The suit claims these practices violate the Sherman Act and Clayton Act by discouraging users from visiting publisher websites, potentially causing publishers to lose 20-40% of their traffic, and seeks an injunction requiring consent for using publisher data to train AI models.

## Key Points

- Helena World Chronicle filed a class action antitrust suit against Google and Alphabet in the U.S. District Court in D.C.
- The lawsuit alleges Google uses AI technologies like SGE and Bard to misappropriate publisher content and reduce traffic to news sites.
- Plaintiffs claim Google's actions violate the Sherman Act and Clayton Act, seeking monetary and injunctive relief.
- Reports indicate publishers may lose 20-40% of website traffic when Google's AI products fully roll out.
- Google previously agreed to pay Canadian media $73.5 million annually, but negotiations with Meta remain unresolved.

## Facts and Data

- The lawsuit was filed in the U.S. District Court in D.C.
- Helena World Chronicle owns two weekly newspapers in Arkansas.
- Google's Knowledge Graph was launched in May 2012.
- By 2020, the Knowledge Graph contained 500 billion facts about 5 billion entities.
- Google drives nearly 40% of The Atlantic's traffic.
- A Wall Street Journal report found AI would answer queries 75% of the time without a click-through.
- Publishers expect to lose 20-40% of website traffic from Google's AI rollout.
- Google links people to publisher websites more than 24 billion times monthly.
- Google paid $73.5 million (100 million Canadian dollars) to Canadian news organizations.
- Meta began blocking news in Canada in August.
- The suit cites a 2023 report from the News Media Alliance regarding AI training data.
- News and Media sites were the third largest category of AI training data according to the Washington Post.
- The Washington Post worked with researchers at the Allen Institute for AI.
- The case references the Epic Games lawsuit against Google over app store antitrust issues.
- The suit asks for an injunction requiring consent to use publisher data for AI training.
- Publishers cannot block SGE because it uses the same web crawler as GoogleBot.
- The law firm handling the case is Hausfeld.
- Google's parent company is Alphabet Inc.
- The suit also references the 2020 Justice Department civil antitrust suit over search advertising.
