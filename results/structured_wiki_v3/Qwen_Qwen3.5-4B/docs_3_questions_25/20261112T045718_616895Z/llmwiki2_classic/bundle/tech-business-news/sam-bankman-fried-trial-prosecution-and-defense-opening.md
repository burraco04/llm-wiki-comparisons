During the opening statements before witnesses took the stand, Prosecutor Thane Rehn accused Sam Bankman-Fried of stealing billions from thousands of victims through Alameda Research and FTX, using Caroline Ellison as a front for secret access to customer funds. Rehn alleged that Bankman-Fried stole funds to finance jet-setting, donate millions to political candidates, and finance risky bets.

Defense lawyer Mark Cohen countered by arguing that Bankman-Fried acted in good faith as a nerdy startup founder and did not defraud anyone. Cohen characterized Alameda Research as a successful hedge fund and FTX as a very innovative, successful company rather than a Ponzi scheme. He stated that Alameda acted legally as an FTX customer, payment processor, and market maker. Cohen used the analogy that "working at a startup is like building a plane as you're flying it" and blamed Ellison for not adequately protecting the hedge fund from crypto market risks. Cohen concluded that Bankman-Fried started and built two billion-dollar businesses and did not steal any money.

The prosecution subsequently called witnesses Marc-Antoine Julliard and Adam Yedidia. Julliard is a Paris-born cocoa trader living in London who invested in crypto in 2021, chose FTX as his exchange, and traded Bitcoin and Dogecoin. Gary Wang is scheduled to testify next.
