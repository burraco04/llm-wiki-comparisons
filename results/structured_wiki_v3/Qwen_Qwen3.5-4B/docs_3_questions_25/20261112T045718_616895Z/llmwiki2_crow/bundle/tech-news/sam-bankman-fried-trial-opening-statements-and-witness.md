Sam Bankman-Fried, former CEO of crypto exchange FTX, was the defendant in a trial where lawyers presented conflicting narratives regarding his character and actions. The jury was selected on Wednesday morning before prosecutors and defense attorneys exchanged opening statements.

Prosecutor Thane Rehn opened with a 30-minute statement alleging Bankman-Fried stole customer funds to finance a jet-setting lifestyle, donate millions to political candidates, and finance risky bets. Rehn claimed Bankman-Fried used Alameda Research, a hedge fund he owned, and his girlfriend Caroline Ellison (CEO of Alameda) as a front to gain secret access to customer money (cash and crypto). Rehn alleged Bankman-Fried directed employees to conceal money flows into FTX and forged financial documents for lenders and investors. Rehn characterized Bankman-Fried not as a 'crypto boy' genius but as a 'man' who stole billions from thousands of victims.

Defense attorney Mark Cohen argued Bankman-Fried did not defraud anyone and acted in 'good faith' as a nerdy startup founder, contrasting this with the prosecution's 'cartoon of a villain' narrative. Cohen referenced Bankman-Fried's appearance after spending seven weeks in a Brooklyn prison.

In his defense opening, Sam Bankman-Fried described Alameda as a successful hedge fund, not a Ponzi scheme, arguing it acted legally as an FTX customer, payment processor, and market maker. He used the analogy that 'working at a startup is like building a plane as you're flying it,' blaming former Alameda CEO Ellison for failing to protect the fund from crypto market risks. SBF claimed he was willing to give up personal wealth to make customers whole and stated, 'He didn't steal any money.'

Witness Marc-Antoine Julliard, a Paris-born cocoa trader living in London, testified in Nov. 2021. He invested in FTX in 2021, trading Bitcoin and Dogecoin. On Nov. 8, he attempted to withdraw almost $100,000 in cash and crypto but was never able to retrieve it.

Witness Adam Yedidia, an MIT graduate and close friend of SBF in college, testified on Nov. 8. After SBF left Jane Street, Yedidia joined him as a trader at Alameda and then as a developer at FTX. Yedidia claimed legal immunity, stating he was concerned he had 'unwittingly written code that contributed to a crime.' Testimony was adjourned at 4:30 p.m. on Nov. 8; Yedidia will continue on Thursday, followed by Matt Huang (former Sequoia Capital partner) and Gary Wang (SBF lieutenant).
