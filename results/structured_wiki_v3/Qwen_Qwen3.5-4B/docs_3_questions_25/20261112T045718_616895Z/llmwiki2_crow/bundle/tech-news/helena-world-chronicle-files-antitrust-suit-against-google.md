Helena World Chronicle, an Arkansas publisher that owns two weekly newspapers, filed a class action lawsuit in the U.S. District Court in D.C. against Google and Alphabet, alleging anticompetitive behavior under the Sherman Act. The suit accuses Google of siphoning off publishers' content, readers, and ad revenue, causing them to lose billions of dollars and starving the free press. The plaintiff specifically cites AI technologies, including Search Generative Experience (SGE) and the Bard AI chatbot, as exacerbating these harms.

The case alleges that Google's Knowledge Graph, launched in May 2012 and containing 500 billion facts by 2020, misappropriated information from publishers' websites and open source databases. Additionally, the suit references a 2023 News Media Alliance report and a Washington Post article stating that Google's Bard AI was trained on news and magazine publications, with news sites being the third largest category of AI training data.

The lawsuit seeks damages and an injunction requiring Google to obtain publisher consent to use website data for training AI products, including those of rivals, and allowing opt-out publishers to appear in search results. The case also cites concerns over changing AdSense rates and Google's destruction of chat messages, an issue raised in the Epic Games antitrust lawsuit which Epic won. Hausfeld law firm states that Google's scheme causes profound harm to competition, consumers, labor, and a democratic free press. The plaintiff invokes the Sherman Act and Clayton Act for relief. The U.S. lawsuit follows a Google agreement with the Canadian government where Google pays $73.5 million annually to Canadian news organizations, while negotiations with Meta remain unresolved after Meta blocked Canadian news in August. The case arrives alongside the U.S. Justice Department's lawsuit against Google for monopolizing digital ad technologies, referencing a 2020 DOJ civil antitrust suit over search and search advertising.
