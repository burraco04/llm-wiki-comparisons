On the second day of Sam Bankman-Fried's trial, prosecutors and defense lawyers presented opening statements before witnesses took the stand. Prosecutor Thane Rehn described Bankman-Fried as a 'man' who stole billions from thousands of victims, contrasting his public image as a 'crypto boy' genius. Rehn alleged Bankman-Fried oversaw a thriving FTX, jetted internationally, and hobnobbed with celebrities like Tom Brady and Larry David while claiming customer funds were safe. Rehn stated Bankman-Fried built his success on lies, allegedly stealing customer funds to finance a jet-setting lifestyle, donate millions to political candidates, and finance risky bets. The prosecution claimed Bankman-Fried used Alameda Research, a hedge fund he owned, and his girlfriend Caroline Ellison (CEO of Alameda) as a front to gain secret access to customer cash and crypto. Prosecutors alleged Bankman-Fried directed employees to conceal money flows into FTX and forged financial documents for lenders and investors.

Defense attorney Mark Cohen argued Bankman-Fried did not defraud anyone and acted in 'good faith' as a nerdy startup founder, rejecting the prosecution's 'cartoon of a villain' narrative. The defense argues Alameda was a successful hedge fund, not a Ponzi scheme, and acted legally as FTX's customer, processor, and market maker. Defense compares startup work to building a plane while flying it, blames Ellison for failing to protect Alameda from crypto market risks. Defense claims Bankman-Fried was willing to give up personal wealth to make customers whole and did not steal money.
