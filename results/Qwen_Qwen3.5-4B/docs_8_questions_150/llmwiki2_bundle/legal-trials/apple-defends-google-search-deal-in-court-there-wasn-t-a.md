In the US v. Google case, Eddy Cue testified that Apple negotiated the 2016 Information Services Agreement (ISA) with Google CEO Sundar Pichai because there was no valid alternative to Google's search engine. Cue stated that Apple initially wanted a higher revenue share, but Google insisted on keeping the deal as is, resulting in a compromise paid since 2016. The ISA, which has been in place since 2002, makes Google the default search engine on Apple devices.

Cue argued that showing unknown alternatives during the iPhone setup process would harm the user experience, noting he could not recall some names of other engines and that users can easily switch providers. He also claimed Apple offers better privacy than Google, citing ISA provisions allowing login-free search and Apple's efforts to hinder tracking. When questioned about the ISA's impact on profits, Cue replied that Apple does not view it that way because the platform's overall appeal makes the agreement successful.

Regarding history, Schmidtlein and Cue argued that Google helped Safari succeed via seamless integration, with early promotional materials highlighting Google. Cue explained that before 2003, users had to type specific URLs like google.com, whereas Apple introduced logic where non-URL text in the URL field automatically searches. While the Department of Justice urged Apple to analyze market impact if checks stopped, Cue stated Apple never considered such market impact. Specific revenue share numbers were discussed in closed court sessions, and Cue maintained Apple never seriously considered switching providers or building its own search product.
