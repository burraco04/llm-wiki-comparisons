# Overview
Sam Bankman-Fried co-founded Alameda Research in 2017 and FTX in 2019 as a complement to it. Within two years of FTX's founding, over 80 investors provided approximately $2 billion in capital. In January 2022, FTX raised $400 million in a Series C round, boosting its valuation to $32 billion. In early November 2022, CoinDesk published Alameda's balance sheet showing $14.6 billion in assets and $8 billion in liabilities as of June 30, 2022. Alameda's largest assets were $3.66 billion of 'unlocked FTT' and $2.16 billion of 'FTT collateral', with Alameda owning $5.82 billion in FTT tokens, which was 193% higher than the total FTT market cap of about $3 billion. In early November 2022, Binance pulled out its remaining $2.1 billion equivalent of cash in BUSD and FTT, triggering a bank run. FTX and Alameda filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in the U.S. in mid-November 2022. Sam Bankman-Fried resigned as CEO and John J. Ray III was appointed the new CEO. At The New York Times' DealBook Summit, Bankman-Fried stated he did not try to commit fraud and was shocked by the events. SBF was arrested in the Bahamas in December 2022, extradited to the U.S., and released on $250 million bail; his house arrest in Palo Alto was revoked in August following a diary leak accusation. Gary Wang and Caroline Ellison pleaded guilty in December 2022 to federal charges and face civil penalties from the SEC and CFTC, planning to cooperate as witnesses. SBF pleaded not guilty in January to wire fraud, conspiracy to commit money laundering, and conspiracy to misuse customer funds, facing a potential sentence of up to 115 years in jail if convicted. The FTX collapse was the first of many, with BlockFi filing for Chapter 11 in November 2022 and Genesis Global Trading filing in January.

Michael Lewis's book 'Going Infinite' chronicles the rise and fall of SBF and is set to debut on the first day of the trial in Manhattan Federal Court. Lewis claims he was introduced to SBF in early 2021 by a friend who wanted to back FTX; the friend asked Lewis to meet the young billionaire in Berkeley to 'evaluate his character'. Before any financial commitment, SBF did raise some $2 billion, including $1 billion in 2021, from backers that included Sequoia, Temasek, Tiger Global and dozens more. Lewis himself was so taken with the idea of SBF and his apparent brilliance that he came away with the decision to write about him. The episode has fired up a lot of people, indignant at what they feel was too-easy treatment for someone who many believe brought down the whole crypto house.
