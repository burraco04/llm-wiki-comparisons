Helena World Chronicle, an Arkansas-based publisher owning two weekly newspapers, filed a class action antitrust suit in the U.S. District Court in D.C. against Google and Alphabet. The suit alleges that Google violates U.S. antitrust laws by siphoning news publishers' content, readers, and ad revenue, thereby starving the free press and causing publishers to lose billions of dollars.

The lawsuit specifically targets Google's Search Generative Experience (SGE) and Bard AI chatbot as technologies worsening the problem for publishers. It also targets Google's Knowledge Graph, launched in May 2012, citing its role in anticompetitive behavior. The Knowledge Panel, which displays summaries drawn from the Knowledge Graph database to the right of search results, was compiled by Google by extracting information from publishers' websites and open source/licensed databases. By 2020, the Knowledge Graph contained 500 billion facts about 5 billion entities, much of which the complaint alleges was misappropriated from publishers.

Additionally, the suit cites Google's 'Featured Snippets' technology, which algorithmically extracts answers from webpages, as shifting traffic away from publishers. A Wall Street Journal report noted that when The Atlantic modeled Google integrating AI into search, 75% of the time AI would answer queries without a click-through to the publisher's site. Plus cites a 2023 News Media Alliance report and a Washington Post article stating Google's Bard AI was trained on news, magazine, and digital publications, noting news sites were the third largest category of AI training data.

The case cites concerns over changing AdSense rates and Google's destruction of chat messages, an issue raised in the Epic Games antitrust lawsuit which Epic won. The suit requests an injunction requiring Google to obtain publisher consent to use website data for training AI products and allows publishers opting out of SGE to appear in search results. Google reached a Canadian agreement paying $73.5 million (100 million Canadian dollars) annually to news organizations based on headcount; negotiations with Meta are unresolved after Meta blocked Canadian news in August.

The U.S. lawsuit follows a Justice Department suit against Google for monopolizing digital ad technologies and references a 2020 DOJ civil antitrust suit over search and search advertising. Hausfeld law firm states Google's anticompetitive scheme harms competition, consumers, labor, and a democratic free press. A Google spokesperson called the lawsuit meritless, claiming Google links people to publisher websites more than 24 billion times monthly at no cost.
