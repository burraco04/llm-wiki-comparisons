Google maintains a dominant position in search, driven by extensive data collection on user searches, smart speaker interactions, clicked results, and ads. This scale, longevity, and ubiquity allow Google to personalize results more effectively than competitors like Bing, which utilizes generative AI but lacks functional follow-up capabilities. While switching providers is temporarily frustrating due to Google's memory of familiar sites, users frequently bypass Microsoft's default search on new PCs, and many returned to Google after Mozilla's Yahoo default on Firefox.

Legal and ethical challenges persist. The US must prove Google's status quo violates the law, presenting an uphill battle. Allegations of manipulation for ad revenue, such as those raised by Megan Grey (former DuckDuckGo VP), suggest Google may prioritize monetizable subjects. Google denies pushing users toward specific topics, stating ad systems do not affect organic results and that keyword matching is a standard, separate process. Conversely, Wired removed an article on manipulation, yet its widespread sharing indicates public suspicion of privacy invasions. Furthermore, search quality has improved significantly as data volume grew from 9 ZB in 2013 to 120 ZB in 2023, shifting from static sites to dynamic information within services and apps.
