Helena World Chronicle, an Arkansas-based publisher owning two weekly newspapers, filed a class action antitrust suit against Google and Alphabet in the U.S. District Court in D.C. The filing, dated this week, alleges violations of the Sherman Act and other antitrust laws regarding Google's anticompetitive behavior.

The plaintiff asserts that Google "siphons off" news publishers' content, readers, and ad revenue, effectively "starving the free press" and causing publishers to lose billions of dollars. The suit specifically targets Google's AI initiatives and data practices:

*   **Search Generative Experience (SGE) and Bard:** GoogleBot, used by SGE, crawls publisher sites without allowing blocks, keeping users in Google's ecosystem while "plagiarizing" content. The suit cites a Washington Post report stating Bard AI was trained on news, magazine, and digital publications, with news sites being the third-largest category of training data.
*   **Knowledge Graph:** Launched in May 2012, this feature displays Knowledge Panels derived from web materials and open-source databases. By 2020, the database contained 500 billion facts about 5 billion entities. The suit alleges the misappropriation of "collective intelligence" from publishers.
*   **Featured Snippets:** These algorithmically extract answers from webpages, shifting traffic away from publishers.

A Wall Street Journal report cited in the suit notes that if Google integrated AI into search, AI could answer 75% of queries without click-throughs to publisher websites. The case also references concerns over changing AdSense rates and Google's destruction of chat messages, an issue previously raised in the Epic Games antitrust lawsuit.

The suit seeks damages and an injunction requiring Google to obtain publisher consent to use website data for training AI products (including those of rivals) and to allow publishers opting out of SGE to appear in search results. Helena World Chronicle invokes the Sherman Act and Clayton Act to seek class-wide relief. This U.S. lawsuit follows a Canadian agreement where Google pays $73.5 million annually to news organizations based on headcount, though negotiations with Meta remain unresolved. The case arrives alongside the U.S. Justice Department's lawsuit against Google for monopolizing digital ad technologies, referencing a 2020 DOJ civil antitrust suit over search and search advertising. The plaintiff argues Google's scheme causes profound harm to competition, consumers, labor, and a democratic free press.
