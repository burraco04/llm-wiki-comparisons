On the second day of the trial, prosecutor Thane Rehn opened with allegations that Sam Bankman-Fried, former CEO of FTX, stole customer funds to finance jet-setting, donate millions to political candidates, and finance risky bets. Rehn claimed Bankman-Fried used Caroline Ellison, CEO of Alameda Research, as a front for 'secret access' to customer money (cash and crypto). Rehn alleged Bankman-Fried directed employees to conceal money flows into FTX and forged financial documents. Rehn characterized Bankman-Fried as a 'man' who 'stole billions of dollars from thousands of victims,' contrasting him with the 'crypto boy genius' narrative.

Defense attorney Mark Cohen opened by stating 'Sam didn't defraud anyone.' Cohen argued Bankman-Fried was a 'nerdy startup founder' who acted in 'good faith,' rejecting the prosecution's 'cartoon of a villain' narrative.

In subsequent testimony, SBF described Alameda as a successful hedge fund and FTX as a 'very innovative, successful company,' arguing business practices were reasonable. He claimed Alameda acted legally as an FTX customer, payment processor, and market maker. SBF used the analogy that 'working at a startup is like building a plane as you're flying it,' noting businesses sometimes fail. He blamed Ellison, former CEO of Alameda, for not adequately protecting the hedge fund from crypto market risks. SBF stated he was willing to give up personal wealth to make customers whole. He concluded: 'Sam started and built two billion-dollar businesses' and 'didn't steal any money.'

The prosecution called Marc-Antoine Julliard, a Paris-born cocoa trader living in London. In 2021, Julliard invested in crypto and chose FTX, trading Bitcoin and Dogecoin. On Nov. 8, he tried to withdraw almost $100,000 but never succeeded.

The government then called Adam Yedidia, an MIT graduate and close friend of SBF. After SBF left Jane Street, Yedidia joined him as a trader at Alameda and developer at FTX. Yedidia testified he had 'unwittingly written code that contributed to a crime' and received legal immunity.

Court adjourned at 4:30 p.m. Yedidia will continue testimony on Thursday, followed by Matt Huang (former Sequoia Capital partner) and Gary Wang (SBF lieutenant).
