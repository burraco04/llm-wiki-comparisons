Sam Bankman-Fried co-founded Alameda Research in 2017 and FTX in 2019. FTX's valuation peaked at $32 billion within a few years of 2019, supported by over 80 investors providing approximately $2 billion in capital in the same period. In January 2022, FTX raised $400 million in its last public funding round (Series C). That same year, FTX purchased the naming rights for the Miami Heat's home arena.

In early November 2022, CoinDesk published Alameda's balance sheet, which showed $14.6 billion in assets and $8 billion in liabilities as of June 30, 2022. The balance sheet included $3.66 billion of 'unlocked FTT' and $2.16 billion of 'FTT collateral'. Alameda owned $5.82 billion in FTT tokens, representing 193% of the total FTT market cap of about $3 billion at the time. Shortly after, in early November 2022, Binance withdrew $2.1 billion equivalent in cash (BUSD and FTT), triggering a bank run on FTX.

FTX and Alameda filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in the U.S. in mid-November 2022. Sam Bankman-Fried resigned as CEO, and John J. Ray III was appointed the new CEO. SBF maintained his innocence and expressed shock regarding the events of November 2022.

The legal proceedings continued with SBF's arrest in the Bahamas in December 2022, followed by extradition to the U.S. He was released on $250 million bail, and his house arrest in Palo Alto was revoked in August following a diary leak accusation. During testimony, John J. Ray III stated that FTX's House Financial Services had virtually no internal controls, no separateness, and did not trust a single piece of paper. U.S. Attorney Damian Williams characterized the crimes as the biggest financial fraud in American history.

Gary Wang and Caroline Ellison pleaded guilty in December 2022 to federal charges and face civil penalties from the SEC and CFTC, planning to cooperate as witnesses. SBF pleaded not guilty in January to charges including wire fraud, conspiracy to commit money laundering, and conspiracy to misuse customer funds, facing up to 115 years in jail if convicted on all charges. The FTX collapse was the first of many, with BlockFi filing for Chapter 11 in November 2022 and Genesis Global Trading filing in January.
