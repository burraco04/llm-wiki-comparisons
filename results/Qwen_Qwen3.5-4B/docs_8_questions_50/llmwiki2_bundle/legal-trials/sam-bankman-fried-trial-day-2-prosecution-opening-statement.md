On the second day of the trial, prosecutor Thane Rehn opened with the claim that Bankman-Fried, former CEO of FTX, appeared to be on top of the world one year prior, overseeing a thriving crypto exchange, traveling internationally, and associating with celebrities like Tom Brady and Larry David. Rehn stated that Bankman-Fried repeatedly assured customers their money was safe. Rehn declared that all of this was built on lies and that behind the scenes, Bankman-Fried was not what he appeared. The prosecution presented a roughly 30-minute story alleging Bankman-Fried stole customer funds to facilitate his jet-setting lifestyle, donate millions to political candidates, and finance risky bets. The key to the alleged scheme was Alameda Research, a crypto hedge fund Bankman-Fried owned, which used Caroline Ellison, his girlfriend and CEO of Alameda, as a front. The government claimed Bankman-Fried had secret access to customer money, both cash and crypto. Rehn alleged Bankman-Fried directed employees to conceal the flow of money into FTX's coffers and forged financial documents distributed to lenders and investors.
