Google collects user searches, smart speaker interactions, clicks on results and ads, enabling effective personalization across search, mobile widgets and other services. Users frequently use the Google Search bar to return to familiar sites rather than just searching for new information, making switching providers temporarily annoying. Kent Walker, Google's president of global affairs, stated Google is the favorite of users, browsers and device makers because it is the best, and argued default status is not determinative. Users actively restore Google as default when it is not set, such as when Mozilla made Yahoo! default on Firefox or when setting up new devices. Google and its competitors are not on an even playing field, raising questions about whether user preference is due to scale, longevity, ubiquity or data collection.

Bing overhauled its layout to be graphic and augmented by generative AI chatbot explanations for follow-up questions, though this feature is not yet fully functional. Bing Chat offered expensive bike comparisons for 'kids BMX bike green' and failed to filter results under $200, returning only options over $300. Wired removed an article on Google Search manipulation, though its social sharing and coverage elsewhere show public willingness to accept foul play on Google's part regarding privacy and data handling. Megan Grey, former DuckDuckGo VP, admits possible evidence misinterpretation but maintains Google manipulates Search to maximize ad revenue; Google denies pushing users toward monetizable subjects. Danny Sullivan states ad systems do not affect organic results (blue links); keyword matching connects people to ads, while a separate process matches organic results to queries using meaning matching for spelling errors and synonyms.

Global data creation grew from 9 ZB/year in 2013 to 120 ZB/year in 2023, shifting from static websites to live information nested in apps, complicating indexing. Google's central index exceeds 100 million gigabytes; Search uses quick processes to determine query meaning, machine learning to rank results by quality and usability, and optionally uses user location and web history if enabled. Google claims it does not infer race or religion to shape results and ran nearly 900,000 quality tests in 2022; critics like Megan Grey contend semantic matching widens content pools to increase ad auctions.
