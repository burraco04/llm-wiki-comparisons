Helena World Chronicle, which owns two weekly Arkansas newspapers, filed a class action lawsuit in U.S. District Court in D.C. against Google and Alphabet, accusing them of violating the Sherman Act and Clayton Act. The suit alleges that Google is starving the free press by sharing publishers' content on Google, causing them to lose billions of dollars. The lawsuit specifically cites AI technologies, including Search Generative Experience (SGE) and the Bard AI chatbot, as tools used to siphon news publishers' content, readers, and ad revenue through anticompetitive means.

The case references Google's Knowledge Graph, launched in May 2012, which grew to contain 500 billion facts about 5 billion entities by 2020. Much of this data was allegedly misappropriated from publishers' websites and open source databases. The suit also cites a 2023 News Media Alliance report and a Washington Post article stating that Google's Bard AI was trained on news, magazine, and digital publications, with news sites being the third-largest category of AI training data.

The case highlights concerns over changing AdSense rates and Google's destruction of chat messages, which the plaintiffs characterize as improper spoliation of evidence, referencing the recent Epic Games antitrust lawsuit where Epic won. The suit seeks damages and an injunction requiring Google to obtain publisher consent to use website data for training AI products, including rivals' products, and allowing publishers opting out of SGE to appear in search results.

The U.S. lawsuit follows a Google agreement with the Canadian government to pay Canadian media $73.5 million annually, distributed by headcount, while negotiations with Meta remain unresolved after Meta blocked Canadian news in August. The case arrives alongside the U.S. Justice Department's lawsuit against Google for monopolizing digital ad technologies, referencing a 2020 DOJ civil antitrust suit over search and search advertising. Hausfeld law firm states that Google's anticompetitive scheme harms competition, consumers, labor, and a democratic free press.
