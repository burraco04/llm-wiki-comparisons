# News publisher files class action antitrust suit against Google, citing AI’s harms to their bottom line

## Core Content

A class action lawsuit has been filed by the Helena World Chronicle against Google and Alphabet, alleging anticompetitive practices and the negative impact of AI technologies on news publishers' revenues and content distribution.

## Key Points

- Helena World Chronicle files a class action lawsuit against Google and Alphabet
- Claims Google siphons off news publishers' content, readers, and ad revenue
- Specifically targets AI technologies like SGE and Bard AI
- Argues these technologies worsen the problem of content misappropriation
- Requests an injunction to prevent Google from using publishers' content for AI training

## Facts and Data

- Google receives approximately 40% of publishers' traffic
- 75% of queries answered by AI do not require clicking to publishers' websites
- Google's Knowledge Graph database includes 500 billion facts about 5 billion entities
- News publishers expect to lose between 20-40% of their traffic when AI products fully roll out
- Google paid $73.5 million annually to Canadian news organizations under a recent agreement
- Meta began blocking news in Canada in August due to the need to pay for content under new Canadian laws
- Google's AdSense rates may change due to AI advancements
- Google destroyed chat messages related to the lawsuit, raising concerns about improper spoliation of evidence
