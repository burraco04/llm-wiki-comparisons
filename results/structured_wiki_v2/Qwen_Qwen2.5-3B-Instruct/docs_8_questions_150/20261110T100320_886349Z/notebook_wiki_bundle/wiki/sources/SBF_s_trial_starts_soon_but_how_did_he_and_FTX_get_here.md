# SBF’s trial starts soon, but how did he — and FTX — get here?

## Core Content

This document discusses the start of Sam Bankman-Fried's criminal trial for fraud and conspiracy related to FTX, a bankrupt cryptocurrency exchange. It traces the rise and fall of FTX, highlighting its rapid growth, celebrity endorsements, and eventual collapse due to liquidity issues and regulatory concerns.

## Key Points

- Sam Bankman-Fried is facing trial for fraud and conspiracy related to FTX
- FTX was founded by Bankman-Fried in 2019 and peaked at $32 billion in valuation
- FTX faced a liquidity crisis and collapsed in early 2022
- Bankman-Fried maintains his innocence but faces multiple criminal charges
- FTX and Alameda Research, Bankman-Fried's trading firm, filed for bankruptcy

## Facts and Data

- FTX was founded by Sam Bankman-Fried in 2019
- FTX reached a peak valuation of $32 billion in 2022
- Alameda Research received over $2 billion in capital from investors
- FTX had a balance sheet showing assets significantly higher than the market value of its tokens
- Binance pulled out $2.1 billion in BUSD and FTT from FTX
- FTX filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in mid-November 2022
- FTX's collapse led to the bankruptcy of BlockFi and Genesis Global Trading
- FTX's collapse affected billions of crypto assets in legal proceedings
- Bankman-Fried maintains his innocence but faces multiple criminal charges
- FTX and Alameda Research's former CEO, Caroline Ellison, pleaded guilty to federal charges
- FTX's collapse caused significant damage to the crypto industry
- Bankman-Fried is represented by Cohen & Gresser and Mark Cohen, a high-profile defense attorney
- FTX's collapse resulted in the loss of billions of dollars for investors and creditors
- FTX's collapse highlighted the risks associated with unregulated cryptocurrency exchanges
