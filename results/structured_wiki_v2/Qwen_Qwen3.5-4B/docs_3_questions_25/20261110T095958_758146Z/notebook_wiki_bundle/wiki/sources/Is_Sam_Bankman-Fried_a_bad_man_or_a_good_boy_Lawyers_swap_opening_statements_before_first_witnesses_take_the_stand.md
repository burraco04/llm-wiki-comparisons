# Is Sam Bankman-Fried a bad ‘man’ or a good ‘boy’? Lawyers swap opening statements before first witnesses take the stand

## Core Content

The second day of the Sam Bankman-Fried trial featured starkly contrasting opening statements where prosecutors painted him as a liar who stole billions for a lavish lifestyle, while his defense argued he was a good-faith startup founder who acted reasonably. The day concluded with the testimony of victim Marc-Antoine Julliard and former friend Adam Yedidia, who revealed his concerns about unwittingly contributing to the alleged crimes through code he wrote.

## Key Points

- Prosecutors alleged Bankman-Fried stole customer funds to finance his lifestyle, political donations, and risky bets via Alameda Research.
- Defense attorneys argued Bankman-Fried acted in good faith, built successful businesses, and did not defraud anyone.
- Witnesses included a victim who lost nearly $100,000 and a former friend who testified due to legal immunity regarding code he wrote.
- The trial featured high-profile attendees including Bankman-Fried's parents, U.S. Attorney Damian Williams, and venture capitalist Matt Huang.
- The prosecution emphasized that Bankman-Fried was a 'man' who stole billions, while the defense portrayed him as a 'boy' genius.
