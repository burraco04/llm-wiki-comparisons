On the second day of the trial, prosecutors led by Thane Rehn opened with a 30-minute statement characterizing Sam Bankman-Fried as a 'man' who stole billions from thousands of victims. They alleged he built FTX on lies, using Alameda Research and Caroline Ellison as fronts for secret access to customer funds, forging documents, and lying to the world. The prosecution highlighted his lavish lifestyle, including international travel, celebrity associations, and political donations.

Defense attorney Mark Cohen countered that Bankman-Fried did not defraud anyone, portraying him as a 'nerdy startup founder' acting in 'good faith' rather than a 'cartoon of a villain.' Cohen noted that Bankman-Fried spent about seven weeks in a Brooklyn prison.

During the defense's opening, Bankman-Fried defended Alameda as a successful hedge fund and FTX as an innovative company, not a Ponzi scheme. He claimed Alameda operated legally as a customer, processor, and market maker, using the analogy that 'working at a startup is like building a plane as you're flying it.' He blamed Ellison for failing to protect the fund from market risks and stated he was willing to give up personal wealth to make customers whole, concluding, 'Sam started and built two billion-dollar businesses,' and 'He didn't steal any money.'

The first prosecution witness was Marc-Antoine Julliard, a Paris-born cocoa trader living in London. Julliard testified that he invested in FTX in 2021, trading Bitcoin and Dogecoin, and failed to withdraw almost $100,000 on November 8.
