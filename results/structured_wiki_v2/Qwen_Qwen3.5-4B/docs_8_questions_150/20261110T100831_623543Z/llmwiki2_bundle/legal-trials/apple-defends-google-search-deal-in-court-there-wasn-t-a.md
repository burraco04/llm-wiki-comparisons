In the US v. Google case, Eddy Cue testified that Apple had no valid alternative to Google's search engine at the time of the 2016 renegotiation. Cue stated that Apple protects user privacy and believes in Google, arguing there was no valid alternative to Google's search engine. He negotiated the 2016 iteration of the Information Services Agreement (ISA) with Sundar Pichai regarding revenue share; the ISA, in place since 2002, makes Google the default search engine on Apple devices. Cue testified that Apple never seriously considered switching to another provider or building its own search product, stating there still isn't a valid alternative. Apple argued that Google is the best choice and switching is easy, despite ISA restrictions, and claimed better privacy than Google by making Safari harder to track. Apple downplayed Google's financial contribution as a profit driver. The DOJ's case focuses on deals Google makes with Apple, Samsung, Mozilla, and others to ensure it is the default search engine on practically every platform, questioning if Google is the best engine or just pays the most. Google integration was a key feature of Safari since before 2003. Apple and Google argue that switching search engines is easy and a non-issue.
