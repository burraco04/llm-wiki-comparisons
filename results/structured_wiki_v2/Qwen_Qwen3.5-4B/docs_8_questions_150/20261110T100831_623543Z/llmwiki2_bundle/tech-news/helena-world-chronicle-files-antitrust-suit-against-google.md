Helena World Chronicle, an Arkansas-based publisher owning two weekly newspapers, filed a class action antitrust suit against Google and Alphabet in the U.S. District Court in D.C. on behalf of news publishers. The suit alleges Google violates the Sherman Act and other U.S. antitrust laws by siphoning off publishers' content, readers, and ad revenue through anticompetitive means. The complaint specifically cites Google's AI technologies, including Search Generative Experience (SGE) and the Bard AI chatbot, as worsening the problem of content misappropriation.

The suit identifies Google's Knowledge Graph, launched in May 2012, as part of the problem, noting it extracts information from publishers' websites and open source databases. By 2020, the Knowledge Graph contained 500 billion facts about 5 billion entities, much of which was allegedly misappropriated from publishers. Google's Featured Snippets, which algorithmically extract answers from webpages, are cited as shifting traffic away from publishers' websites. A Wall Street Journal report noted that when The Atlantic modeled Google integrating AI into search, 75% of the time AI would answer queries without a click-through to the publisher's site. Google currently drives nearly 40% of The Atlantic's traffic according to Similarweb data, and publishers believe they will lose 20-40% of their website traffic when Google's AI products fully roll out.

Plus cites a 2023 News Media Alliance report and a Washington Post article stating Google's Bard AI was trained on news, magazine, and digital publications, noting news sites were the third-largest category of AI training data. The case cites concerns over changing AdSense rates and Google's destruction of chat messages, improper spoliation of evidence raised in the Epic Games antitrust lawsuit which Epic won. The suit requests an injunction requiring Google to obtain publisher consent to use website data for training AI products and allows publishers opting out of SGE to appear in search results.

Google reached a last-month agreement with the Canadian government to pay $73.5 million (100 million Canadian dollars) annually to news organizations based on headcount; negotiations with Meta remain unresolved after Meta blocked Canadian news in August. The U.S. lawsuit follows the Justice Department's 2020 civil antitrust suit over search and search advertising, distinct from the recent suit over digital ad technologies. Hausfeld law firm states Google's anticompetitive scheme harms competition, consumers, labor, and a democratic free press; Plaintiff Helena World Chronicle, LLC invokes the Sherman Act and Clayton Act. A Google spokesperson called the lawsuit meritless, claiming Google links people to publisher websites more than 24 billion times monthly at no cost.
