Google's market dominance is attributed to its scale, longevity, ubiquity, and extensive user data collection, which includes searches, smart speaker interactions, clicks on results, and ad interactions. This data enables effective personalization across services, though it creates an uneven playing field for competitors. Users frequently rely on Google's default status, often switching back to it after changes by other providers like Yahoo! or Microsoft, with 'Google' remaining the top search query on Bing in 2021.

Google's search engine maintains a central index exceeding 100 million gigabytes, updated constantly by crawling software to prioritize high-quality sites. The system distinguishes between organic results (blue links) and ads, with Danny Sullivan clarifying that ad keyword matching does not alter organic rankings. However, controversies exist regarding search manipulation; former DuckDuckGo VP Megan Grey has claimed Google manipulates results to maximize ad revenue, a stance Google denies. Additionally, Bing has faced criticism for its generative AI features and pricing failures, such as returning results over $300 for a query about a 'kids BMX bike green' under $200.

Google defends its practices, with Kent Walker stating that users actively choose Google over alternatives and that default status is not determinative. The company conducts nearly 900,000 quality tests annually with independent raters and denies inferring race or religion from user data. Meanwhile, global data creation has surged from 9 ZB per year in 2013 to 120 ZB in 2023, shifting the web from static sites to live information nested in services. Despite these dynamics, Wired removed an article on Google Search manipulation, though public discourse elsewhere suggests continued scrutiny of Google's data handling.
