# Is Sam Bankman-Fried a bad ‘man’ or a good ‘boy’? Lawyers swap opening statements before first witnesses take the stand

## Core Content

The second day of the Sam Bankman-Fried trial featured starkly contrasting opening statements where prosecutors painted him as a liar who stole billions for a lavish lifestyle, while his defense argued he was a good-faith startup founder who acted innovatively. The day concluded with the testimony of victim Marc-Antoine Julliard and former friend Adam Yedidia, who revealed his concerns about unwittingly contributing to the alleged crimes through code he wrote.

## Key Points

- Prosecutors alleged Bankman-Fried stole customer funds to finance his lifestyle, political donations, and risky bets via Alameda Research.
- Defense attorneys argued Bankman-Fried acted in good faith, built successful businesses, and did not defraud anyone.
- Witnesses included a victim who lost nearly $100,000 and a former friend who admitted to writing code that may have contributed to the scheme.
- The trial featured high-profile attendees including Bankman-Fried's parents, U.S. Attorney Damian Williams, and venture capitalist Matt Huang.
- Bankman-Fried spent seven weeks in a Brooklyn prison before this trial, resulting in more prominent cheekbones.

## Facts and Data

- Sam Bankman-Fried was the former CEO of FTX.
- The trial took place on the second day.
- Thane Rehn was the prosecutor for the government.
- Mark Cohen was one of Bankman-Fried's lawyers.
- Damian Williams is the U.S. attorney for the Southern District of New York.

- Marc-Antoine Julliard is a Paris-born cocoa trader living in London.
- Julliard invested in Bitcoin and Dogecoin on FTX.
- Julliard tried to withdraw almost $100,000 on November 8.
- Adam Yedidia is a graduate of MIT.
- Yedidia was a close friend of Bankman-Fried in college.

- Yedidia has legal immunity during his testimony.
