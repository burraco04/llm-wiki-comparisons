# 5 things we learned so far about the Google antitrust case

## Core Content

Google CEO Sundar Pichai testified in a U.S. antitrust trial defending the company's $26.3 billion spending to secure default search engine status across platforms, including a major deal with Apple. The Department of Justice argues these tactics block competition and hinder rivals from accessing necessary data, drawing parallels to past antitrust cases against Microsoft and Google's own historical stance on default settings.

## Key Points

- Google spent $26.3 billion in 2021 to become the default search engine on various platforms.
- The Department of Justice claims Google's dominance hinders competitors by blocking access to advertising and search data.
- Google agreed not to promote Chrome to Safari users as part of its deal with Apple.
- Pichai admitted attempting to get Google preinstalled on iPhones in 2018, which Apple declined.
- The trial highlights Google's hypocrisy regarding default search engine settings compared to its past actions against Microsoft.

## Facts and Data

- Google paid $26.3 billion in 2021 to make itself the default search engine.
- Approximately $18 billion of the payment went directly to Apple.
- Google's deal with Apple has been in place since 2003.
- When users switch their browser homepage to Google, they perform 15% more searches.
- When users switch away from Google, their searches drop by 27%.
- Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella offered to spend $15 billion annually to make Bing the default on Apple devices.
- Google gets 16 times more data than Bing every day.
- The DOJ accused Google of systematically destroying chat messages via the history-off option.
- Pichai changed the history-off default for chat in February to comply with a litigation hold.
- The trial is expected to continue for several weeks.
- The case is compared to the 1990s U.S. trial against Microsoft.
- If ruled against Google, the outcome could resemble the Microsoft settlement.
- Google might be required to turn over collected data to other search engines.
- The FTC sued Amazon in September for anticompetitive strategies.
- The DOJ has been investigating Apple for years over its third-party app policies.
- There is an ongoing case between the FTC and Facebook regarding Instagram and WhatsApp.
- Google's legal chief David Drummond sent an angry letter to Microsoft in 2005.
- The letter accused Microsoft's Internet Explorer default setting as anticompetitive.
- Google's internal chat logs were deleted despite being subject to a litigation hold.
- Pichai testified on October 28 regarding the search head's revelations.
- The DOJ filed a separate claim in January alongside eight states.
