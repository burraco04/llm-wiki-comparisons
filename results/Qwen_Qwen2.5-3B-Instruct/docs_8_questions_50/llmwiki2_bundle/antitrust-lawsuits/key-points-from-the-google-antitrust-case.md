# Key Points from the Google Antitrust Case

## Google's Spending on Being Default Search Engine
- In 2021, Google spent $26.3 billion to be the default search engine across various platforms, with Apple receiving $18 billion of this amount.

## Google's Deal with Apple
- Google agreed not to promote Chrome to Safari users as part of its search deal with Apple, which prevented Apple from switching to a Google competitor or allowing users to choose their browser.

## Google's Attempt to Preinstall Services on iPhones
- In 2018, Google CEO Sundar Pichai attempted to convince Apple CEO Tim Cook to preinstall Google services on every iOS device, aiming for a win-win situation where Google gains more searches and data, while Apple earns from a lucrative search agreement.

## Google's Chat Log Deletion Policy
- Google deleted internal chat messages automatically every 24 hours, except when manually changed. This practice was criticized by the Department of Justice (DOJ) during Pichai's testimony, leading to a litigation hold and subsequent changes in the policy.

## Google's Antitrust Comparison to Microsoft
- David Drummond, Google's legal chief, sent an angry letter to Microsoft in 2005, arguing that making Internet Explorer the default search engine was anticompetitive. This argument is now used by other search engines like DuckDuckGo, Brave, and Bing against Google, accusing it of becoming a monopoly.
