Helena World Chronicle, LLC filed a class action lawsuit in U.S. District Court in D.C. against Google and Alphabet, invoking the Sherman Act and Clayton Act. The suit alleges anticompetitive behavior where Google and Alphabet siphon off content, readers, and ad revenue through AI technologies, including Search Generative Experience (SGE), Bard, and the Knowledge Graph launched in May 2012.

The Knowledge Graph grew to 500 billion facts about 5 billion entities by 2020. The lawsuit alleges Google compiled this database by extracting information from publishers' websites and open source/licensed databases, misappropriating content that 'starves the free press' and costs publishers billions of dollars. A Wall Street Journal report cited by the suit states that if Google integrated AI into search, 75% of the time AI would answer queries without a click-through to publisher sites, potentially causing publishers to lose 20-40% of traffic; currently, Google drives nearly 40% of publisher traffic.

Plus cites a 2023 News Media Alliance report and a Washington Post article stating Google's Bard AI was trained on news, magazine, and digital publications, noting news sites were the third-largest category of AI training data. The case alleges changing AdSense rates and improper spoliation of evidence via Google's destruction of chat messages, referencing the recent Epic Games antitrust win.

The suit seeks damages and an injunction requiring Google to obtain publisher consent to use website data for training AI products, including rivals' AI, and allowing opt-out publishers to appear in search results. Google reached a Canadian agreement paying $73.5 million (100 million CAD) annually to news organizations based on headcount; negotiations with Meta remain unresolved after Meta blocked Canadian news in August. The U.S. lawsuit follows a Justice Department suit against Google for monopolizing digital ad technologies and references a 2020 DOJ civil antitrust suit over search and search advertising.

Hausfeld law firm states Google's scheme causes profound harm to competition, consumers, labor, and a democratic free press. A Google spokesperson called the lawsuit meritless, claiming Google links people to publisher websites more than 24 billion times monthly at no cost.
