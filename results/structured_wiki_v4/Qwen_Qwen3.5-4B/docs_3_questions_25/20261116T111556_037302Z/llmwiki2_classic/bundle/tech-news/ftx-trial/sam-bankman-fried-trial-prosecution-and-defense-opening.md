In the Sam Bankman-Fried trial, Prosecutor Thane Rehn accused Bankman-Fried of stealing billions from thousands of victims through Alameda Research and FTX, using Caroline Ellison as a front for secret access to customer funds. The prosecution alleged that Bankman-Fried stole funds to finance jet-setting, donate millions to political candidates, and finance risky bets, while forging financial documents and concealing money flows.

Defense attorney Mark Cohen countered that Bankman-Fried acted in good faith as a nerdy startup founder and did not defraud anyone, rejecting the prosecution's 'cartoon of a villain' narrative. Cohen argued that Alameda was a successful hedge fund and FTX was an innovative, successful company, not a Ponzi scheme. He stated that Alameda acted legally as an FTX customer, payment processor, and market maker, and used the analogy that 'working at a startup is like building a plane as you're flying it.' Cohen blamed Ellison for not adequately protecting the hedge fund from crypto market risks and concluded that Bankman-Fried started and built two billion-dollar businesses without stealing any money. Prosecution witnesses Marc-Antoine Julliard and Adam Yedidia testified, with Gary Wang scheduled to testify next.
