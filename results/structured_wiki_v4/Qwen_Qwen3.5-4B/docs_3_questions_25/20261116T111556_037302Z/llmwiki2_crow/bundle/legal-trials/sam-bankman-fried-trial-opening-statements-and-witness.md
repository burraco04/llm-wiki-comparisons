Sam Bankman-Fried, former CEO of crypto exchange FTX, was the defendant in a trial where the jury was selected on Wednesday morning. On the second day of the trial, prosecutors and defense lawyers presented opening statements before witnesses took the stand.

Prosecutor Thane Rehn described Bankman-Fried as a 'man' who stole billions from thousands of victims, contrasting his public image of safety with alleged lies. Rehn alleged Bankman-Fried used Alameda Research, a hedge fund he owned, and his girlfriend Caroline Ellison, CEO of Alameda, as a front to gain secret access to customer funds. The prosecution claimed Bankman-Fried stole customer money to finance a jet-setting lifestyle, donate millions to political candidates, and finance risky bets. Rehn alleged Bankman-Fried directed employees to conceal money flows into FTX and forged financial documents for lenders and investors.

Defense lawyer Mark Cohen argued Bankman-Fried did not defraud anyone and acted in 'good faith' as a nerdy startup founder. Cohen noted Bankman-Fried had spent about seven weeks in a Brooklyn prison before the trial. Damian Williams, U.S. attorney for the Southern District of New York, was present in the audience during the trial.

Sam Bankman-Fried described Alameda as a successful hedge fund and FTX as an innovative company, not a Ponzi scheme. He argued Alameda acted legally as an FTX customer, payment processor, and market maker. SBF used the analogy that 'working at a startup is like building a plane as you're flying it' and claimed businesses sometimes fail. He blamed Ellison, former CEO of Alameda, for not adequately protecting the hedge fund from crypto market risks. When facing legal trouble, SBF allegedly offered to give up personal wealth to make customers whole.

Prosecution witnesses included Marc-Antoine Julliard, a Paris-born cocoa trader living in London, who invested in FTX in 2021. Julliard attempted to withdraw almost $100,000 on Nov. 8 but never succeeded. Adam Yedidia, an MIT graduate and SBF college friend, testified with legal immunity. Yedidia claimed he wrote code he believed contributed to a crime. Yedidia joined Alameda after SBF left Jane Street and later worked as a developer at FTX. Court adjourned at 4:30 p.m.; Yedidia will continue testimony on Thursday, followed by Matt Huang (former Sequoia Capital partner) and Gary Wang (SBF lieutenant).
