Helena World Chronicle filed a class action lawsuit in U.S. District Court in D.C. accusing Google and Alphabet of anticompetitive behavior under the Sherman Act. The suit alleges that Google siphons off publisher content, readers, and ad revenue through AI and older technologies.

The complaint specifically cites Google's Knowledge Graph, launched in May 2012, and new AI tools like Search Generative Experience (SGE) and the Bard AI chatbot as worsening the misappropriation of publisher content. By 2020, the Knowledge Graph contained 500 billion facts about 5 billion entities, much of which was allegedly extracted from publishers' websites and open source databases.

The suit alleges that Google's 'Featured Snippets' and other algorithmic extraction methods shift traffic away from publishers' websites. A Wall Street Journal report noted that a model by The Atlantic found 75% of the time AI would answer queries without a click-through to publisher sites, while Google currently drives nearly 40% of publisher traffic.

Additionally, the case points to changing AdSense rates and improper spoliation of evidence by Google's destruction of chat messages, an issue raised in the recent Epic Games lawsuit against Google over app store antitrust issues which Epic won. The suit requests an injunction requiring Google to obtain publisher consent to use website data for training general AI products and to allow publishers who opt out of SGE to still show in Google search results.

Contextually, Google reached a last-month agreement with the Canadian government to pay Canadian media for content use, providing $73.5 million (100 million Canadian dollars) annually distributed based on news outlets' headcount; negotiations with Meta are unresolved after Meta blocked news in Canada in August. Plus states Google's Bard AI was trained on news, magazine, and digital publications, citing a 2023 News Media Alliance report and a Washington Post article; the Post found news sites were the third largest category of AI training data.
