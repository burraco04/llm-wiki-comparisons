The trial against former FTX CEO Sam Bankman-Fried began with opening statements on the second day, following jury selection on Wednesday morning. The prosecution, led by Thane Rehn, alleged that SBF stole billions from thousands of victims and lied to the world. They detailed a scheme involving Alameda Research and Caroline Ellison to gain secret access to customer funds, with actions including directing employees to conceal money flows and forging financial documents. The prosecution characterized SBF as a 'man' who built a thriving exchange, traveled internationally, and financed risky bets, claiming all his public appearances were built on lies.

In contrast, defense attorney Mark Cohen presented SBF as a well-meaning startup founder who acted in 'good faith,' arguing he did not defraud anyone. Cohen noted SBF's appearance, which showed more prominent cheekbones after spending seven weeks in a Brooklyn prison, and aimed to present him as anything but the prosecution's 'cartoon of a villain.' SBF himself described Alameda as a successful hedge fund and FTX as an innovative company, not a Ponzi scheme. He argued Alameda acted legally as a customer, payment processor, and market maker, using the analogy that 'working at a startup is like building a plane as you're flying it.' SBF blamed Ellison for not adequately protecting the hedge fund from market risks and claimed he offered to give up personal wealth when the crisis approached, concluding he built two billion-dollar businesses without stealing money.

Witness testimony began with Marc-Antoine Julliard, a Paris-born cocoa trader living in London who invested in FTX in 2021. Julliard testified that he attempted to withdraw almost $100,000 on November 8, 2021, but never succeeded. Adam Yedidia, an MIT graduate and close friend of SBF, testified that he had legal immunity and persuaded SBF to join him as a trader at Alameda and developer at FTX after SBF left Jane Street, citing concern that he had unwittingly written code contributing to a crime. The court adjourned at 4:30 p.m., with Yedidia scheduled to continue testimony on Thursday, followed by Matt Huang and Gary Wang.
