Google Search faces scrutiny regarding its market dominance, data collection practices, and the fairness of its default status. Kent Walker argues Google is preferred because it is the best, not just the default, citing user choice in Mozilla and Windows scenarios. However, users frequently revert to Google after Mozilla set Yahoo as Firefox default and after Microsoft set Bing as default in 2021, indicating displacement friction.

Google collects user searches, smart speaker interactions, clicked results, and ads, favoring DuckDuckGo for privacy despite potential performance issues. Its index is over 100 million gigabytes, using machine learning to rank results by quality and usability. In 2022, Google ran almost 900,000 quality tests with independent raters. Critics like Megan Grey contend that semantic keyword matching reduces precision to widen content pools and increase ad auctions. Megan Grey, former DuckDuckGo VP, admits possible evidence misinterpretation but maintains Google manipulates Search to maximize ad revenue.

Danny Sullivan states that ad systems do not affect organic results (blue links); keyword matching connects people to ads, while a separate process matches organic results to queries using meaning matching. Conversely, Bing Chat failed to filter results for 'kids BMX bike green' under $200, returning only options over $300, and lacks functional follow-up questions despite an overhaul with generative AI. Global data creation grew from 9 ZB/year in 2013 to 120 ZB in 2023, shifting from static websites to live information nested in apps. The US faces an uphill battle proving Google broke the law to achieve its market status quo, while others gain insight into Google's market share usage. Wired removed an article on Google Search manipulation, though social sharing and coverage elsewhere show public willingness to accept foul play and suspicion of privacy invasion.
