Sam Bankman-Fried (SBF) is the disgraced cryptocurrency entrepreneur accused of orchestrating the multibillion-dollar collapse of FTX, the crypto exchange he founded and led. SBF pleaded not-guilty to some seven charges of fraud and conspiracy in Manhattan Federal Court. He reportedly offered $5 billion to Donald Trump to cancel his presidential re-run bid, though he also looked to back candidates on the Democratic side of the aisle and met with Mitch McConnell to discuss funding Republican candidates to offset Trump's influence.

SBF raised approximately $2 billion, including $1 billion in 2021, from backers that included Sequoia, Temasek, Tiger Global, and dozens more. Michael Lewis's book 'Going Infinite' is set to debut on the first day of SBF's trial; Lewis was introduced to SBF in early 2021 by a friend who wanted to back FTX, asked to meet SBF in Berkeley to 'evaluate his character,' and met SBF more than 100 times over the next two years. Lewis claimed SBF had $22.5 billion eighteen months earlier and was the richest person in the world under 30, and 60 Minutes got involved with the story, shooting a segment with SBF and Lewis.

FTX's trading volume climbed to $15 billion per day. To lend legitimacy, Tom Brady was paid $55 million and Steph Curry $35 million for marketing deals. Anna Wintour contacted SBF via Zoom to ask him to sponsor her Met Costume Ball. SBF's eyes moved back and forth during a TV appearance while trying to win a video game simultaneously.

The hard crash occurred as FTX cryptocurrency valuations fell sharply after being based on speculation rather than banked value. SBF is denied bail, blamed by crypto investors for losses at Alameda/FTX, and accused of causing a ripple effect of crypto woes. Ayelet Noff, CEO of Sliced Brand, states SBF lacked management experience, was over his head, and delayed crypto adoption by at least one to two years. Michael Lewis's interview with SBF is criticized for defending him as an 'effective altruist' and claiming FTX had a great real business, despite no proof of idealism. FTX failure resulted from financial discrepancies between the exchange and its sister company Alameda Research, a trader on the platform.
