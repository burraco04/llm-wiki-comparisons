The U.S. government's antitrust trial against Google stems from a 2020 claim regarding its dominance in the online search market, separate from a January filing by the Department of Justice and eight states to halt Google's anticompetitive scheme. The government argues Google uses platforms and deals to block competition in search or advertising, hindering competitors from accessing data needed to improve products. Google argues its actions are legal business practices because being the default engine is desirable for consumers.

Google CEO Sundar Pichai testified in the trial, defending tactics including deals to make Google the default search engine. He stated the decision to pay for defaults was made with the consumer in mind to gather data and be the best search engine across devices. On October 28, Google search head Prabhakar Raghavan testified that Google spent $26.3 billion in 2021 to make itself the default search engine across platforms, including Apple, Samsung, and Mozilla. About $18 billion of this total went directly to Apple, per a New York Times report. Google's deal with Apple has been in place since 2003.

Historical context includes a 2007 Google product strategy meeting email showing that when users changed their browser homepage to Google, they did 15% more Google searches, dropping 27% when they switched away. In 2005, Microsoft made Internet Explorer the default search engine, prompting Google's legal chief David Drummond to send an angry letter accusing this of anticompetitive behavior. In 2018, Pichai admitted attempting to get Tim Cook to preinstall Google on every iOS device to create inseparable services, but Cook refused as Apple does not preload third-party software. Additionally, the DOJ accused Google of systematically destroying chats via a history-off option deleting messages every 24 hours; Pichai claimed he took action against the history-off default in February to comply with the DOJ's litigation hold.
