Eddy Cue testified in the US v. Google antitrust trial defending the Information Services Agreement (ISA), which has made Google the default search engine on Apple devices since 2002. In 2016, Cue negotiated the current iteration of the ISA with Google CEO Sundar Pichai; while Apple sought a higher revenue share and Pichai wanted to maintain the status quo, they compromised on an undisclosed percentage.

Cue argued there was no valid alternative to Google at the time and none exists now, stating Apple never seriously considered switching providers or building its own search product. He emphasized that the deal was about more than economics. Regarding user experience, Cue acknowledged the ISA did not allow Apple to offer search engine choices during setup, citing speed and user experience as reasons for defaulting to Google.

On privacy, Cue agreed Apple values user privacy and claimed Apple offers better privacy than Google, citing ISA provisions for login-free searches and anti-tracking measures. When questioned about the ISA's impact on Apple's profits, Bellshaw asked if it represented a significant portion; Cue replied that Apple does not view it that way because the platform's overall appeal makes the agreement successful. Schmidtlein led Cue through Safari's history, noting Google integration was a user interface innovation mentioned in pre-2003 promotional materials. Cue explained that before 2003, users had to type specific URLs to search, whereas Apple's system redirects non-URL text to search. Schmidtlein and Cue argued that Google helped Safari succeed as a great product and that switching search engines is easy, referencing Apple's deals with Yahoo and Bing. Finally, in response to DOJ questions about whether Google is the best search engine or simply pays the most, and what the market looks like without those payments, Cue stated Apple never considered these factors.
