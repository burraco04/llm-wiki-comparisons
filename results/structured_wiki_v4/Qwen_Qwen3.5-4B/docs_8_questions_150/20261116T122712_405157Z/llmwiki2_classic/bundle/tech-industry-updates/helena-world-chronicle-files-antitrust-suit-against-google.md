Helena World Chronicle, LLC filed a class action lawsuit in U.S. District Court in D.C. against Google and Alphabet, invoking the Sherman Act and Clayton Act to seek class-wide monetary and injunctive relief. The suit alleges that Google and Alphabet engage in anticompetitive behavior by siphoning off content, readers, and ad revenue through AI technologies such as Search Generative Experience (SGE) and Bard, as well as older tools like the Knowledge Graph, which was launched in May 2012.

The Knowledge Graph grew to 500 billion facts about 5 billion entities by 2020. The lawsuit alleges Google compiled this database by extracting information from publishers' websites and open source/licensed databases, misappropriating content that 'starves the free press' and costs publishers billions of dollars. A Wall Street Journal report cited by the suit states that when The Atlantic modeled Google integrating AI into search, 75% of the time AI would answer queries without a click-through to the publisher's site. Since Google drives nearly 40% of publisher traffic, publishers expect to lose 20-40% of their website traffic when Google's AI products fully roll out.

The case cites concerns over changing AdSense rates and Google's destruction of chat messages, referencing the recent Epic Games antitrust lawsuit where Epic won. The suit seeks damages and an injunction requiring Google to obtain publisher consent to use website data for training AI products (including rivals' AI) and allowing opt-out publishers to appear in search results. Plus cites a 2023 News Media Alliance report and a Washington Post article stating Google's Bard AI was trained on news, magazine, and digital publications, noting news sites were the third-largest category of AI training data.

The U.S. lawsuit follows a Google agreement with the Canadian government to pay $73.5 million (100 million Canadian dollars) annually to Canadian news organizations based on headcount, while negotiations with Meta remain unresolved. Meta began blocking news in Canada in August due to pressure from the new Canadian law to pay for content. The case arrives alongside the U.S. Justice Department's lawsuit against Google for monopolizing digital ad technologies, referencing a 2020 DOJ civil antitrust suit over search and search advertising. Hausfeld law firm states Google's anticompetitive scheme causes profound harm to competition, consumers, labor, and a democratic free press.
