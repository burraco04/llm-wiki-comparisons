Google Search is frequently favored by users due to its ability to personalize mobile widgets and services through extensive data collection on searches, smart speaker interactions, and ad clicks, a capability Bing lacks. While Bing has recently overhauled its layout with generative AI and offered comparative search results (e.g., for 'kids BMX bike green'), it has faced criticism for returning options over $300 when asked for those under $200, whereas DuckDuckGo ignored the color specification in the query. Kent Walker, Google's president of global affairs, argues that Google is the preferred choice of users, browsers, and device makers because it is the best in business, noting that switching providers is easy and users often revert to Google as default. He claims the industry is competitive and that users choose Google voluntarily rather than being forced. Despite Google's market share advantages stemming from its scale, longevity, and user data, proving it broke the law to maintain this status quo in the US is described as an uphill battle. Controversies include allegations that Google manipulates search results to maximize ad revenue, a claim admitted by former DuckDuckGo VP Megan Grey (though she may have misinterpreted evidence) and supported by a Wired article that was later removed for not meeting standards. Google's official response, provided by Danny Sullivan, distinguishes between long-standing ad keyword matching and separate organic result matching systems that handle meaning and spelling. Additionally, search quality is argued to have improved since 2013 due to a massive increase in data volume from 9 ZB to 120 ZB, driven by live information in apps rather than static websites.
