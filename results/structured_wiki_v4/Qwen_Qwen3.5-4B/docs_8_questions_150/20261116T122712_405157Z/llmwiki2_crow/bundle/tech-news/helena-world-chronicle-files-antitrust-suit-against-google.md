Helena World Chronicle, an Arkansas-based publisher owning two weekly newspapers, filed a class action antitrust suit in the U.S. District Court in D.C. against Google and Alphabet on behalf of news publishers. The suit alleges that Google violates the Sherman Act and other U.S. antitrust laws by siphoning off publishers' content, readers, and ad revenue through anticompetitive means, thereby starving the free press and causing publishers to lose billions of dollars.

The lawsuit specifically targets Google's Search Generative Experience (SGE) and Bard AI chatbot, citing them as technologies that worsen the problem for publishers. It also targets Google's Knowledge Graph, launched in May 2012, alleging that much of the 'collective intelligence' within it was misappropriated from publishers. The Knowledge Graph, compiled by extracting information from publishers' websites and open source/licensed databases, contained 500 billion facts about 5 billion entities by 2020. Google's Knowledge Panel displays summaries drawn from this database to the right of search results, and its 'Featured Snippets' technology, which algorithmically extracts answers from webpages, is cited as shifting traffic away from publishers. A Wall Street Journal report noted that when The Atlantic modeled Google integrating AI into search, 75% of the time AI would answer queries without a click-through to the publisher's site.

Plus cites a 2023 News Media Alliance report and a Washington Post article stating that Google's Bard AI was trained on news, magazine, and digital publications, with news sites being the third-largest category of AI training data. The case alleges changing AdSense rates and improper spoliation of evidence via Google's destruction of chat messages, an issue raised in the recent Epic Games antitrust lawsuit which Epic won. The suit seeks damages and an injunction requiring Google to obtain publisher consent to use website data for training general AI products, including Google's own and rivals', and allows publishers opting out of SGE to appear in search results.

The U.S. lawsuit follows a last-month agreement where Google pays Canadian media $73.5 million (100 million Canadian dollars) annually, distributed by headcount, after Meta began blocking Canadian news in August under new laws. The case arrives alongside the U.S. Justice Department's lawsuit against Google for monopolizing digital ad technologies, referencing a 2020 DOJ civil antitrust suit over search and search advertising. Hausfeld law firm states that Google's anticompetitive scheme harms competition, consumers, labor, and a democratic free press. A Google spokesperson called the lawsuit meritless, claiming Google links people to publisher websites more than 24 billion times monthly at no cost.
