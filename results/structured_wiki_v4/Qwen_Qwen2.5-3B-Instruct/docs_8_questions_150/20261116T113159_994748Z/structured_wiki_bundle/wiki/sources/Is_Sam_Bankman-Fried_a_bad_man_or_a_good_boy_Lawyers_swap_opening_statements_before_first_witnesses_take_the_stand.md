# Is Sam Bankman-Fried a bad ‘man’ or a good ‘boy’? Lawyers swap opening statements before first witnesses take the stand

## Core Content

The trial focuses on whether Sam Bankman-Fried, former CEO of FTX, is a liar and fraud or a well-meaning founder. The prosecution portrays him as a criminal who stole billions from thousands of victims, while his defense argues he acted in good faith.

## Key Points

- Trial examines Sam Bankman-Fried's role in FTX
- Prosecution accuses him of stealing from customers
- Defense claims he acted in good faith, not a criminal
- Witnesses include a crypto trader and a college friend
- Court adjourns after calling first two witnesses

## Facts and Data

- FTX was once a thriving crypto exchange
- Bankman-Fried jetted between international locations
- Ellison, his girlfriend and Alameda CEO, had secret access to customer funds
- Bankman-Fried allegedly lied to customers about safety
- Prosecution alleges Bankman-Fried used Alameda to steal from FTX
- Bankman-Fried reportedly donated millions to political candidates
- Prosecution claims Bankman-Fried concealed money flows and forged documents
- Bankman-Fried allegedly used Alameda as a front to steal from FTX
- Bankman-Fried claimed he gave up personal wealth to make customers whole
- Julliard invested almost $100,000 in FTX but never recovered
- Yedidia was a college friend of Bankman-Fried and a former Alameda developer
- Huang and Wang are expected to testify next
- Ellison is a key witness for the prosecution
