# 5 things we learned so far about the Google antitrust case

## Core Content

This document discusses the ongoing antitrust trial against Google, focusing on the company's practices and defenses. Key points include Google's payments to secure default search engine status, attempts to preinstall Google services on Apple devices, and the deletion of internal chat logs. The case highlights the struggle between Google's dominance and the need for fair competition.

## Key Points

- Google paid $26.3 billion in 2021 to be the default search engine across various platforms.
- Google attempted to get Apple to preinstall Google services on iPhones but failed.
- Google deleted internal chat logs during a litigation hold, leading to controversy.
- The case is expected to continue for several weeks, potentially affecting other tech giants' practices.
- The outcome could require Google to share data with competitors and alter its business practices.

## Facts and Data

- Google spent $26.3 billion in 2021 to secure its position as the default search engine.
- Apple received $18 billion of Google's $26.3 billion payment in 2021.
- Google agreed not to promote Chrome to Safari users as part of its search deal with Apple.
- Google attempted to preinstall Google services on iPhones in 2018 but was unsuccessful.
- Google deleted internal chat logs during a litigation hold, raising concerns about data retention policies.
