# SBF, riding high on FTX, reportedly offered $5B to Trump to cancel President re-run

## Core Content

This document discusses Sam Bankman-Fried (SBF) and his involvement with FTX, focusing on the upcoming trial and the book 'Going Infinite' by Michael Lewis. It also touches on SBF's alleged attempts to buy off Donald Trump and his controversial interviews.

## Key Points

- Sam Bankman-Fried faces trial for orchestrating the collapse of FTX
- Michael Lewis wrote a book about SBF's rise and fall
- SBF allegedly offered $5 billion to Trump to cancel the presidential runoff
- SBF's wealth and influence grew rapidly, leading to lucrative marketing deals
- FTX's collapse resulted in significant financial losses for users

## Facts and Data

- SBF pleaded not guilty to multiple charges of fraud and conspiracy
- Lewis was introduced to SBF in early 2021 by a friend seeking to invest in FTX
- Lewis claims he was initially not planning to write a book about SBF
- FTX's daily trading volume peaked at $15 billion
- SBF raised $2 billion from investors including Sequoia, Temasek, and Tiger Global
- SBF attempted to secure political support for his crypto ventures
- The value of cryptocurrencies on FTX plummeted after the exchange's collapse
- SBF's company, Alameda Research, was linked to financial discrepancies
- SBF is accused of misleading investors and potentially manipulating the market
- The book 'Going Infinite' is set to debut during SBF's trial
- SBF's wealth increased from nothing to $22.5 billion in just 18 months
- SBF's appearance on 60 Minutes was described as nervous and strategic
- Anna Wintour reportedly reached out to SBF for sponsorship of her Met Costume Ball
